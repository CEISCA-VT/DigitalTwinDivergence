\section{Source Estimator and Data}
The source model settings below are the frozen settings used to generate
the saved trajectories. Delivery analysis does not refit the source model.
The fast branch has two GRU layers with 64 hidden units, inter-layer
dropout 0.10, and a 20-sample history at 10~Hz. Its six features are
odometer speed, IMU yaw rate, odometer acceleration, IMU yaw acceleration,
absolute yaw rate, and absolute odometer acceleration. The slow branch
has two tanh layers of 32 units and a 300-sample history. Its 16 features
are mean, standard deviation, RMS, and mean magnitude of both IMU and
wheel yaw rate, mean, standard deviation, and RMS of wheel--IMU yaw
disagreement, mean and standard deviation of normalized disagreement,
and mean, standard deviation, and mean magnitude of forward speed.
Slow correction is zero until the full history is available.

The slow target is the training-only causal 30-s mean of the reference
yaw correction. The fast target is the remainder. The objective weights
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:losses}. AdamW uses learning rate $10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-5}$, batch size eight, at most 25 epochs, and
early-stopping patience five. Training and validation strides are 50
and 100 samples. Normalization uses training recordings and checkpoint
selection uses validation recordings. The speed and fast/slow yaw
correction limits are 0.15~m/s and 0.020/0.005~rad/s.

timestamp. Satisfaction is averaged across three capture realizations
and three model seeds before the sequence-level decision is made.

\section{Planning Formulation}
Let receiver-time blocks begin at 30~s and have 100 evaluation samples,
except for a possible shorter final block. The four action indices
represent rates 10, 5, 2, and 1~Hz, with strides 1, 2, 5, and 10 samples.
An arrival occurs at each block boundary and then at its selected stride.
Each source timestamp is the arrival time minus fixed latency $L$.
For the evaluated conditions, $L$ is 0, 100, 200, or 300~ms. This arrival
phase, rather than just the maximum horizon, makes the local factorization
exact: a held state depends on the action in its receiver-time block.
The two endpoints of any supported motion interval are in the same or
adjacent blocks.

Create binary edge variables $z_{b,p,c}$ indicating previous action $p$
and current action $c$ in block $b$. Only $z_{0,0,c}$ exists in the first
block, where the first zero is a placeholder. Start and flow constraints
are
\begin{align}
\sum_c z_{0,0,c}&=1,\\
\sum_a z_{b-1,a,p}&=\sum_c z_{b,p,c},\quad b\geq1.
\end{align}
These constraints choose one action per block with consistent neighboring
actions. No scored local endpoint precedes the prefix because local
scoring begins after its complete horizon.

For every edge and service, independently replay its affected queries
to obtain the integer count $C_{s,b,p,c}$ of joint passes summed across
the three seeds. Let $N_s$ be the number of service queries in one seed.
The required service constraints are
\begin{equation}
\sum_{b,p,c} C_{s,b,p,c}z_{b,p,c}
\geq \left\lceil3N_s\eta_s\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For a block with $n_b$ evaluation samples and current rate $r_c$, its
cost is $c_{b,c}=\lceil n_b r_c/10\rceil$. Minimize
\begin{equation}
\sum_{b,p,c}c_{b,c}z_{b,p,c}.
\end{equation}
This includes the final partial block and counts updates in the scored
receiver window. Shared prefix traffic is excluded from all policies.
With fixed latency and no loss, every counted arrival corresponds to
one transmission shifted earlier by $L$.

For each of ten recordings, two source banks, and two network delays,
the six service sets are each service individually, all three local
services together, and all four together. This gives 240 problems.
The mixed-integer optimization solver minimizes the stated integer cost
with a zero relative-gap target.
The initial limit is ten seconds per problem. Four time-limited feasible
cases were refined with 60-second limits. The stored solver bound and
incumbent cost determine the reported optimality gap. All returned
schedules are independently replayed against the original service checks.

The fixed comparator tries each of the four constant actions and retains
the cheapest qualifying one. The greedy comparator starts from each
qualifying fixed schedule, lowers a single block to the next lower rate,
and retains only changes preserving every requested contract. Candidate
changes are ordered by normalized satisfaction loss per saved update,
then by larger update saving and earlier block index. The least-cost
completed greedy schedule is retained. These algorithms use the same
reference outcomes, seed averaging, cost convention, and block family.

\section{Optimistic Bound and Its Domain}
At query $v$, a candidate 10-Hz source state must have timestamp
$\tau\leq v-L$ and age at most 1.5~s. The current endpoint additionally
must meet the service-specific age bound. At a historical endpoint,
the 1.5-s class cap applies, but the current-endpoint freshness limit
is not imposed retrospectively. Historical and current timestamps
are ordered, and a candidate pair is shared across seeds.

All planning queries have available reference values, and the admissible
schedule class retains a receiver state at every required endpoint.
Thus, the query set and satisfaction denominator are fixed. At each query,
maximize the number of seeds passing jointly over all admissible choices.
An empty set contributes zero. Every realizable schedule selects one of
these choices, so its seed-averaged indicator is bounded by this maximum.
Averaging proves the manuscript's upper bound. Allowing choices at
different queries to be inconsistent only enlarges the set and preserves
the inequality. The certificate applies to this fully observable,
timestamp-ordered, bounded-age class on the native source clock.

The audit enumerates 64, 256, 1024, and 4096 schedules for three, four,
five, and six blocks, respectively, with latency 0, 100, 200, and 300~ms.
Each case ends in a 37-sample partial block. Direct replay verifies every
factor count and update cost, and every replay result is below the
optimistic bound. Exact minima and infeasibility agree with enumeration
for all six requested service sets.

\section{Screening and Sensitivity Audits}
The outer test recording is excluded from the source pipelines generating
development trajectories as well as from screen fitting and cutoff
selection. Inner cutoff cross-fitting refits only the screen over those
fixed development trajectories. For development recording $j$, its source
pipeline excludes $j$ and the outer test. Other development recordings can
have contributed to that source fit. This dependency differs from fitting
a new source bank for every inner screening split.

The cutoff minimizes the difference between requested and development
acceptance, keeps equal scores together, and chooses the higher cutoff
when the differences tie. Table~\ref{tab:service_screening} reports the
close predictors' actual service composition under the frozen cutoffs.
Each service has 200 outer-test cells per bank. Complete per-method,
per-recording counts and the joint-satisfaction shortfall of every
accepted failure are in the saved audit CSV files.
