\title{Cross-Sequence Service Qualification and Delivery Remediability for Frozen IoT Digital Twins}

\author{Shreyas Udaya%
\thanks{S. Udaya is with the Bradley Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Virginia Tech, Blacksburg, VA, USA.}%

\begin{abstract}
An IoT digital twin may support one service while failing another because services require different quantities, horizons, discrepancy limits, and freshness. We present a causal, held-out qualification-and-remediability protocol for frozen twins. Thirty saved trajectories from ten i2Nav physical sequences are replayed over sampled delivery configurations. A cross-sequence qualification surface estimates, from nine sequences, whether each service and delivery cell qualifies on the tenth. At a target acceptance of 30\%, the surface falsely qualifies 10/226 accepted conditions (4.4\%) versus 82/200 (41.0\%) for a service-identity-only baseline. The paired physical-sequence bootstrap difference is $-36.6$ percentage points (95\% interval $-45.4$ to $-26.8$); achieved acceptance differs by 3.25 points, so this is an approximately matched pooled comparison, not a per-service win. AoI-only, an adapted kinematic-staleness score, and motion-conditioned logistic regression are secondary comparisons. Paired replay distinguishes discrepancy persistent under ideal available delivery from additional held-state discrepancy: 31 of 40 sequence--service cases are delivery-remediable, six persist, and three already qualify under degraded delivery. A practical 5-Hz/50-ms sampled configuration restores 28 of the 31. The distinction also appears with a frozen fixed-physics twin. Results characterize qualification transfer and delivery remediability, not an optimal scheduler, safety guarantee, model repair, or measured resource saving.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{igital} twins support monitoring, prediction, and cyber--physical decisions only while their virtual state remains adequate for the service that consumes it. This adequacy is not fully described by an update rate or by one model-centric error. A short-horizon motion service, a global asset tracker, and a geofence can require different quantities, horizons, discrepancy limits, and source ages \cite{dtc,jones2020,shao2023,nist8620}. A twin can consequently remain locally useful while accumulated drift invalidates global synchronization.

Freshness research provides principled age metrics and scheduling formulations. Age of Incorrect Information (AoII) weights the time for which an estimate is wrong \cite{maatouk2023}; Query AoI evaluates freshness when information is requested \cite{chiariotti2022}. Digital-twin synchronization has been formulated as stochastic control balancing update and prediction-bias costs \cite{tanmatta2024}, and as budget allocation for fidelity-aware edge queries \cite{li2025sync}. Recent predictive synchronization combines uncertainty forecasts and drift monitoring to reduce traffic while maintaining prediction quality \cite{wanigarathna2026}. These works decide when or what to update under modeled costs, queries, staleness, or predictive uncertainty. They do not directly answer an empirical qualification question: for a frozen physical--virtual model and a specified service, which delivered rate-delay conditions remain supported on an unseen physical sequence, and which observed failures can fresher delivery actually resolve?

This paper addresses that question with three linked contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Service-relative qualification.} We define pass, fail, and unobservable outcomes from required quantities, horizon, componentwise physical discrepancy, context, and information age. Satisfaction is always reported with observability.
    \item \textbf{Cross-sequence qualification surface.} We causally replay 30 frozen trajectories from ten i2Nav physical sequences over 20 delivery conditions. The qualification frequency of each service--delivery cell is learned without the held-out physical sequence. A service-identity-only baseline tests whether delivery-cell information adds value beyond service difficulty.
    \item \textbf{Delivery-remediability.} Paired replay distinguishes held-state discrepancy from discrepancy persistent under available 10-Hz zero-added-delay delivery. We account for restored, persistent, and already-qualified cases, then repeat the distinction with a documented frozen fixed-physics twin.
\end{enumerate}

At approximately matched pooled acceptance, the surface falsely qualifies 10/226 accepted conditions versus 82/200 for service identity alone. This supports value beyond knowing which service is generally easier; it does not establish a matched within-service win because the identity score is tied across a service's cells. AoI-only remains a freshness reference, while the adapted kinematic and logistic comparisons do not establish superiority over their source methods. Of 31 delivery-remediable sequence--service cases, none is freshness-only; a practical sampled configuration restores 28. We do not claim an optimal scheduler, safety guarantee, model repair, communication saving, or localization state of the art.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Fidelity and Intended Use}
Digital-twin credibility research treats fidelity, validation, synchronization, and uncertainty as use-dependent concerns \cite{jones2020,shao2023,nist8620}. Objective-dependent fidelity \cite{kober2023}, multidimensional evaluation \cite{zhang2025}, trace alignment \cite{munoz2024}, and robot-twin validation measures \cite{bergs2025} all motivate evaluating more than one undifferentiated score. We retain established trajectory errors; the added object is an explicit service qualification and its evidence boundary.

\subsection{Freshness and Synchronization}
AoII models semantic incorrectness over time \cite{maatouk2023}, whereas Query AoI optimizes freshness at pull-based query instants \cite{chiariotti2022}. Tan and Matta formulate DT synchronization as a stochastic control problem and derive state-independent, state-dependent, approximate, and full-information policies for an unreliable production system \cite{tanmatta2024}. Li \emph{et al.} allocate a constrained synchronization budget across objects and evaluate fidelity-aware queries using state staleness and query cost \cite{li2025sync}. Wanigarathna \emph{et al.} use GRU quantile forecasts, coverage checks, and KL-divergence monitoring to switch between predictive and direct synchronization \cite{wanigarathna2026}.

Our study does not replace these scheduling or freshness formulations. In particular, our adapted kinematic-staleness comparator is not a reproduction of Li \emph{et al.}'s multi-object, budget-constrained method \cite{li2025sync}. The contribution is a held-out qualification-and-remediability protocol combining componentwise physical discrepancy, explicit service requirements, observability, causal delivery replay, and paired delivery contrasts. No single ingredient is claimed as new, and we do not claim superiority over general fidelity-aware synchronization methods.

\subsection{Mobile-Robot Twin Evidence}
Robot DTs support experimentation, runtime verification, navigation, and control \cite{ding2021,yang2025,betzer2024}. Wheel--inertial methods provide a sensor-lightweight state representation but remain vulnerable to slip and accumulated heading error \cite{brossard2019,jiang2025,zhou2025ynet}. i2Nav supplies the frozen primary trajectories and independent held-out sequences used here \cite{tang2025}. TerraSentia and the UGV01 experiments are retained as supporting portability and asset-specific boundary evidence \cite{cuaran2024}.

\section{Service Contracts and Qualification Semantics}
Let $z_{p,q}(t)$ and $z_{T,q}(t)$ denote physical and twin values for quantity $q$, with application-appropriate discrepancy
\begin{equation}
D_q(t)=d_q\!\left(z_{p,q}(t),z_{T,q}(t)\right).
\label{eq:discrepancy}
\end{equation}
For service $s$, define the engineering contract
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_s=(Q_s,H_s,\boldsymbol{\tau}_s,C_s,A_s),
\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
where $Q_s$ is the required quantity set, $H_s$ the horizon, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_s$ componentwise discrepancy limits, $C_s$ the applicable context, and $A_s$ the maximum represented-state age.

Let $O_{s,k}$ indicate whether window $W_k$ contains the required reference quantities, history, synchronization, and quality evidence. The outcome is
\begin{equation}
V_{s,k}=\begin{cases}
1, & O_{s,k}=1\ \text{and all limits pass},\\
0, & O_{s,k}=1\ \text{and at least one limit fails},\\
\varnothing, & O_{s,k}=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:threestate}
\end{equation}
For observable windows,
\begin{equation}
V_{s,k}=\mathbb{I}\!\left[g_{q,H_s}(W_k)\le\tau_{s,q}\ \forall q\in Q_s,
c_k\in C_s,\ \mathrm{AoI}_k\le A_s\right],
\label{eq:windowvalidity}
\end{equation}
where $g_{q,H_s}$ is the service-specific discrepancy summary and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AoI}(t)=t-t_{\mathrm{src}}^{\mathrm{newest\ represented}}.
\end{equation}
Run-level satisfaction and observability are
\begin{align}
S_s&=\frac{\sum_k\mathbb{I}[V_{s,k}=1]}{\sum_k\mathbb{I}[V_{s,k}\in\{0,1\}]},\\
\Omega_s&=\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{k=1}^{N_s}O_{s,k}.
\end{align}
An unobservable window is an abstention, not a pass or failure. A violation withdraws only the affected service claim; it does not establish that the entire twin is unusable or unsafe.

For planar robot motion, synchronized position and heading discrepancies are
\begin{align}
D_p(t)&=\sqrt{(x_p-x_T)^2+(y_p-y_T)^2},\\
D_\theta(t)&=|\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_p-\theta_T)|.
\end{align}
Local services use relative $SE(2)$ motion over $H_s\in\{1,5,10\}$~s; the global service compares synchronized pose. Thus local relative-pose error (RPE) and global absolute trajectory error (ATE) remain complementary measurements rather than competing definitions.

\section{Frozen Twin, Data, and Causal Replay}
\subsection{Frozen Prediction Source}
The primary source is the completed i2Nav full leave-one-sequence-out (LOSO) evaluation: ten physical sequences, three frozen V2 seeds per held-out sequence, and 30 saved 10-Hz trajectories. The sensor-lightweight twin propagates corrected odometry speed and IMU yaw rate. No twin retraining, checkpoint selection, alignment, interpolation, or outcome-dependent calibration is performed in this study. Physical and virtual trajectories already share a local ENU frame. Source files and hashes are recorded in the protocol manifest.

Seeds are first aggregated within physical sequence. The statistical hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
\text{timestamp}\subset\text{seed run}\subset\text{physical sequence}\subset\text{dataset}.
\end{equation}
The ten physical sequences are the independent units for uncertainty; replay cells and timestamps are not treated as independent physical experiments.

\subsection{Causal Delivery Replay}
For source times $t_i$, rate reduction deterministically subsamples the recorded 10-Hz virtual stream. Added delay $\delta$ gives arrival time $a_i=t_i+\delta$. At evaluation time $t$, the represented state is the newest source sample satisfying $a_i\le t$. It is held by causal zero-order hold and retains its original source time. No queue is modeled and cached evidence does not become a fresh observation.

Local windows require their complete history. A 30-s prefix supplies sensor-only motion summaries and is excluded from scoring; the remaining suffix supplies outcomes. Each physical sequence therefore contributes $4$ services $\times20$ timing cells, or 80 conditions. Across ten sequences, the matched-acceptance comparison contains 800 evaluable conditions nested within ten physical units.

\section{Cross-Sequence Qualification Surface and Comparators}
\subsection{Cross-Sequence Qualification Surface}
For outer held-out sequence $u$, the empirical score for service $s$, rate $r$, and delay $\delta$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_{-u}(s,r,\delta)=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{v\ne u}
\mathbb{I}[\text{condition }(s,r,\delta)\text{ qualifies on }v].
\label{eq:empiricalbudget}
\end{equation}
This estimates transfer frequency from the other physical sequences without using outcomes from $u$. It preserves separate service surfaces and does not impose a monotone boundary; empirical global-service surfaces contain small nonmonotonicities. The surface is a descriptive held-out estimator, not a learned scheduler or a universal timing law.

\subsection{Comparators}
The \emph{service-identity-only} score is the qualification frequency of service $s$ among the nine outer-training physical sequences, independent of rate and delay. It therefore knows which service is generally easier but cannot distinguish delivery cells within that service. The \emph{AoI-only} score is freshness headroom $A_s-\max\mathrm{AoI}$ and ignores physical discrepancy. The adapted \emph{kinematic-staleness} score is a secondary literature-informed reference using motion and age, not a reproduction of Li \emph{et al.}'s constrained multi-object method \cite{li2025sync}. The \emph{motion-conditioned logistic} comparator receives service requirements, rate/delay, and speed, yaw-rate, acceleration, and turning summaries from the held-out sequence's sensor-only prefix. It receives no target-reference discrepancy. It is class-weighted logistic regression with standardized numeric features and one-hot service identity, trained on the other outer sequences. A common service-agnostic surface is retained only in supplementary outputs.

\subsection{Approximately Matched Acceptance}
A shared numerical score cutoff would be unfair because the five scores have different scales. For each outer fold and method, inner LOSO predictions from the nine outer-training sequences select the cutoff whose training acceptance is closest to 30\%; conservative higher cutoffs resolve ties. The cutoff is frozen and applied to the untouched outer sequence. Selection uses acceptance only, not held-out labels. Achieved acceptance spans 25.0--30.1\% across methods, so the comparison is approximately rather than exactly matched. The service-identity score has only one value per service; its ties can force all-or-none acceptance of that service's cells. Nominal 10\% and 50\% targets do not furnish valid matched-risk evidence for all methods and are reserved for supplementary diagnostics.

False qualification is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FQ}=\frac{\#\{\text{accepted conditions that actually fail}\}}
{\#\{\text{accepted conditions}\}}.
\end{equation}
Every numerator is reported with its accepted-condition denominator. Confidence intervals resample the ten physical sequences with replacement, preserving all within-sequence cells and method pairing. Paired method differences are computed inside each bootstrap draw; significance is not inferred from separate marginal intervals.

We make no superiority claim against Li \emph{et al.} or all fidelity-aware synchronization approaches.

\subsection{Delivery-Remediability and Complete Accounting}
For every one of the $10\times4=40$ sequence--service cases, we compare degraded, practical, and ideal delivery with the same frozen twin and physical trajectory. The partition is exhaustive: 31 cases fail at 2~Hz/200~ms but qualify under 10-Hz zero-added-delay delivery; six remain unqualified under ideal delivery; and three already qualify while degraded. No nonideal grid condition qualifies when ideal delivery fails.

Among the 31 remediable cases, 21 are discrepancy-only failures and ten fail both discrepancy and freshness; none is freshness-only. The practical 5-Hz/50-ms sampled configuration restores qualification in 28/31: 18 discrepancy-only and all ten combined cases. Its represented-state history differs from 5-Hz/0-ms in all 30 saved runs, but 25 and 50 ms alias with each other; we do not attribute recovery to a separately resolved 50-ms delay effect. Fresher sampled delivery reduced the paired held-state discrepancy and restored the specified qualification. It did not recalibrate the twin or repair the robot. The held-minus-ideal discrepancy is descriptive, not an exact additive causal decomposition of model and network error.

\subsection{Frozen-Twin Configuration Sensitivity}
The previously saved deterministic fixed-physics trajectory is a paired configuration check on the same ten sequences, not another sample of physical robots. Fixed physics has 23 delivery-remediable, 16 persistent-under-ideal, and one already-qualified case; frozen V2 has 31, six, and three, respectively. All ten fixed-physics global cases remain persistent under ideal delivery, versus six for V2. Improved delivery restores some qualification for both. Thus the delivery-induced-versus-persistent distinction is not unique to V2, although its counts depend on the frozen twin. V2 has lower mean ideal-delivery position discrepancy across these cases, but its mean held-minus-ideal increment is larger; ideal fidelity does not by itself specify sensitivity to held delivery. These configurations are never pooled as independent experiments.

\section{Supporting Physical and Cross-Domain Evidence}
\subsection{Local Versus Global Fidelity}
The timing study is motivated by a measured local/global distinction. On i2Nav, parking02 has 11.350-m ATE but only 0.019-m RPE1, whereas parking00 has 2.107-m ATE and 0.132-m RPE1. Across the prespecified tolerance surfaces, parking00 is preferred at every global point while parking02 is preferred at every local point. Across all ten sequences, local/global rankings invert in 17/45, 17/45, and 15/45 pairwise comparisons for 1-, 5-, and 10-s services. These pairs share ten sequences and are not independent experiments; the result is an existence demonstration, not a prevalence estimate.

Condition analysis further shows that high wheel--IMU disagreement worsens RPE1 in 10/10 sequences and high turning worsens RPE5/RPE10 in 9/10. Conditioned benign envelopes change sharpness but do not consistently improve held-out coverage; they remain descriptive rather than a superior online predictor. The full local/global counterexample and condition detail are in the supplement.

\subsection{UGV01 Asset-Specific Boundary}
Three UGV01 runs use an elevation-corrected AprilTag/ChArUco reference in a $2.0\,\mathrm{m}\times1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ area. Asset-specific parameters fitted on one run reduce heading MAE from $35.6^\circ$ to $21.2^\circ$, but frozen transfer is unfavorable: heading MAE rises from $28.8^\circ$ to $76.3^\circ$ and from $35.3^\circ$ to $108.7^\circ$ on two holdouts. This is a physical boundary case, not an additional timing trial or a general UGV01 fidelity claim.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Scope and Live Limitation}
The unchanged robot contract remains computable on five TerraSentia sequences, but frozen i2Nav V2 offers essentially no global gain over its physics baseline; this supports contract portability, not model portability. A quantity--horizon--tolerance specialization also yields target-aligned thermal forecast-fidelity evidence on MAGNET \cite{wilsdon2023magnet,inlmagnetdata}. MAGNET lacks verified forecast issuance and availability timestamps, so it supports no causal delivery, freshness, or remediability claim. Other cross-domain feasibility checks are in the supplement.

Twenty UGV01 live runs exercise requested-rate policies, but a serial HTTP bottleneck collapses delivered rates to about 1.5~Hz; low-speed GPS course also limits heading observability. They demonstrate implementation limits, not policy-dependent resource savings. The geofence null result and its sparse-boundary limitation are reported in the supplement.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\subsection{What the Evidence Establishes}
The cross-sequence qualification surface describes which service-specific delivery cells transfer from nine physical sequences to a tenth. The paired replay then distinguishes a failure that fresher sampled delivery can resolve from one that persists with the available source stream. This differs from optimizing a modeled freshness objective: it evaluates specified physical--virtual service outcomes on held-out sequences. It does not constitute a new learned optimizer or a general scheduler.

The result does not establish that 5~Hz/50~ms is universally optimal. It is one predeclared sampled configuration on this grid. Nor does ``remediable'' mean that the model became more physically correct; it means a newer delivered virtual state restored the specified qualification in paired replay. The pooled identity-only advantage is not a matched per-service result: tied service scores prevent that inference.

\subsection{Limitations}
First, requirements are illustrative rather than safety-certified. Second, only ten physical sequences underpin 40 sequence--service cases and 800 nested replay cells; seeds, phases, services, cells, and timestamps are not independent physical experiments. Third, replay changes delivery of saved virtual states rather than packet-level networking or source measurements. Fourth, ideal delivery means the available 10-Hz source stream with zero added delay, not zero model error. Fifth, the saved 10-Hz clock aliases 25- and 50-ms delay histories, and zero-order hold is the primary receiver; causal extrapolation and sampling phase change numerical qualification. The protocol is therefore not implementation-independent. Sixth, global cells show small nonmonotonicities and no monotone boundary is imposed. Seventh, no reference-quality flags exist in the frozen i2Nav trajectory files. Finally, UGV01 calibration does not transfer across two holdouts, the live transport does not realize distinct requested rates, and no communication/energy saving or downstream safety/control improvement has been measured.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The frozen V2 source is associated with commit \texttt{6540c01f90f3c1074de0d8dae9964a5276fbbc91}. The timing protocol, source hashes, 40-case ledger, held-out predictions, matched operating points, paired bootstrap outputs, and plotting code are machine readable. \section{Conclusion}
This paper presents a causal, held-out qualification-and-remediability protocol for frozen IoT digital twins. Explicit service contracts retain discrepancy, horizon, freshness, and observability; the cross-sequence qualification surface estimates transfer for each sampled delivery cell; and paired replay distinguishes delivery-remediable from persistent discrepancy. At approximately matched pooled acceptance, the surface falsely qualifies 10/226 accepted conditions versus 82/200 for service identity alone, a paired difference of $-36.6$ percentage points (95\% interval $-45.4$ to $-26.8$). Thirty-one cases are remediable under ideal available delivery, six persist, and three already qualify while degraded; the distinction also appears under a frozen fixed-physics twin. This is evidence about qualification transfer and delivery remediability, not an optimal scheduler, safety certificate, or measured communication-saving policy.
