\begin{abstract}
Digital-twin (DT) fidelity is often reported as a model-centric score even though an IoT service consumes specific physical quantities over specific horizons, with explicit error and freshness requirements. This paper formulates \emph{service-relative operational fidelity} as an engineering contract over required quantities, service horizon, tolerated discrepancy, operating context, and information age. We connect window validity to run-level contract satisfaction and distinguish deployment qualification from grid-based sensitivity analysis. On frozen i2Nav trajectories, local and synchronized-global service rankings reverse across prespecified tolerance surfaces, while ATE and finite-horizon RPE align most strongly with the services matching their semantics. The same contract construction remains usable on TerraSentia and, through one frozen normalized specialization, on thermal, battery electro-thermal, and biomass-to-SNG DT data. Controlled synchronization replay shows that 50-ms timestamp jitter increases 1-s RPE by 66.2\% while changing ATE by only 0.085\%. AprilTag-referenced UGV01 runs provide physical-asset evidence, including an adverse frozen-parameter cross-run transfer result. In a separate 20-run online UGV01 study, four resource policies produce distinct requested rates but only 1.45--1.52 delivered updates/s because the serial HTTP path is the bottleneck. Measured traces and trace-driven replay thus demonstrate online contract execution and a null delivered-resource effect, not communication savings. The contribution is a mapping from application requirements to measurable fidelity evidence, explicit service decisions, and their observable edge-system limits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{igital} twins are increasingly used as computational references for monitoring, prediction, control, verification, maintenance, and cyber--physical decision making. In an IoT deployment, however, a virtual state is useful only if it is sufficiently credible for the \emph{specific service} that consumes it. A local motion monitor, a global asset tracker, a geofence service, and a long-horizon thermal supervisor do not necessarily require the same state variables, horizons, error limits, operating conditions, or update freshness \cite{dtc,jones2020,shao2023,nist8620}.

This distinction becomes especially visible in sensor-lightweight mobile robots. Wheel/odometry and inertial sensing may preserve short segments of relative motion while slip, heading bias, timestamp error, or accumulated drift displaces the virtual asset globally. ATE can therefore report poor synchronized-global correspondence while short-horizon RPE remains small. The two metrics are not contradictory: they answer different physical questions. The operational ambiguity arises when a paper reports several errors without stating which service each error is supposed to qualify.

We address that ambiguity by treating fidelity as a \emph{service-relative operational contract}. The central hypothesis is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{minipage}{0.88\columnwidth}\centering
DT fidelity is not a scalar property of a twin;\\
it is a service-specific operational claim.
\end{minipage}}
\label{eq:hypothesis}
\end{equation}
The contract associates the quantities required by a service with a horizon, tolerated discrepancy, applicable context, and allowable information age. Established metrics remain the measurements; the contract adds the semantics that convert those measurements into a service-specific qualification or violation.

The contribution is therefore \emph{not} a new trajectory-error metric. It is a formal mapping from application requirements to measurable quantities, time horizons, tolerance rules, freshness constraints, and an explicit operational decision. This distinction matters because a metric can be numerically well defined while the service claim it is supposed to support remains implicit.

This paper is organized around four research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Can the same physical--virtual pair receive different fidelity judgments when the service changes?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Does an explicit service contract add decision-relevant information beyond a single scalar score or ordinary multi-metric reporting?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Does the contract structure remain usable across another robot platform and across non-robot DT domains without outcome-tuned thresholds?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Does physical--virtual timing perturbation affect service horizons differently in a way that matters to edge/IoT deployment?
\end{itemize}

The paper makes four main contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operational fidelity contract and lifecycle.} We formalize a service contract over quantity set, horizon, tolerance, context, and information age, and define how it is specified, qualified, monitored, and handled when violated.
    \item \textbf{Decision-value evidence beyond metric reporting.} A frozen i2Nav study shows threshold-robust local/global ordering reversals, service-specific metric alignment, and sequence-level robustness. The contract therefore makes conventional metrics actionable rather than claiming that they are invalid.
    \item \textbf{Cross-platform and cross-domain structural portability.} The same robot contract transfers unchanged to TerraSentia, and one frozen normalized quantity--horizon--tolerance protocol remains non-degenerate on MAGNET, FreeTwinEV, and TU Wien SNG.
    \item \textbf{Synchronization and physical-asset evidence.} Controlled Gaussian timestamp jitter and fixed update-delay replay show that local services can deteriorate before global ATE reveals a large change. AprilTag-referenced UGV01 runs reveal asset-specific calibration gains and cross-run limits; 20 additional live runs demonstrate online contract and policy execution under a measured transport bottleneck.
\end{enumerate}

The claim boundary is deliberate. We do not claim a universal fidelity threshold, a universal DT certificate, localization state of the art, successful zero-shot transfer of one learned maintenance model across assets, or measured communication savings. The paper evaluates the \emph{operational contract representation and the service decisions it induces}; its live experiment identifies why requested resource modes did not translate into different delivered update rates.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Digital-Twin Fidelity, Credibility, and Intended Use}
DT research consistently identifies fidelity, synchronization, verification, validation, and uncertainty as central credibility concerns \cite{jones2020,shao2023,nist8620}. Kober \emph{et al.} formulate objective-dependent optimum fidelity \cite{kober2023}, while Zhang \emph{et al.} propose multidimensional fidelity evaluation for aero-engine assembly \cite{zhang2025}. These works establish that fidelity depends on purpose and cannot be reduced universally to model accuracy. Our contribution is narrower and operational: we make the consuming service explicit as a contract over quantities, horizon, tolerance, context, and state age.

Mu\~noz \emph{et al.} evaluate physical and digital twins through snapshot similarity and trace alignment \cite{munoz2024}. Trace alignment is valuable when temporal displacement can be tolerated as behavioral equivalence. Our objective is complementary: for services requiring the virtual state to correspond to the physical state \emph{now}, temporal displacement is itself part of fidelity loss. Bergs \emph{et al.} likewise demonstrate the usefulness of multiple robot DT validation measures \cite{bergs2025}. We therefore do not claim novelty from ``using multiple metrics.'' The novelty is the explicit mapping from those measurements to a service-validity decision.

\subsection{Robot Twins and Sensor-Lightweight Maintenance}
Robot DTs have been used for virtual--physical experimentation, navigation/control, and runtime verification \cite{ding2021,yang2025,betzer2024}. Wheel--inertial localization work shows that low-burden proprioceptive sensing can support useful state estimation \cite{brossard2019,jiang2025,zhou2025ynet}. We use that sensing regime because it creates a demanding maintenance problem under drift and changing conditions. The object of study, however, is not an odometry leaderboard; it is whether the resulting physical--virtual pair is credible for an intended service. i2Nav supplies the primary frozen robot study \cite{tang2025}, while TerraSentia provides a substantially different agricultural robot platform \cite{cuaran2024}.

\subsection{Cross-Domain and IoT DT Evaluation}
MAGNET couples a physical heat-pipe experiment with predictive thermal modeling \cite{wilsdon2023magnet,inlmagnetdata}. FreeTwinEV releases controlled electro-thermal measurements and corresponding multiphysics battery simulation data \cite{freetwinevmeas2026,freetwinevsim2026}. The TU Wien biomass-to-SNG pilot-plant DT combines process simulation, control, and soft sensing \cite{jankovic2025sng,tuwiensngdata}. We use these systems not as model-comparison benchmarks but to test whether the same service-contract representation remains meaningful when robot pose and trajectory metrics disappear.

DT-assisted anomaly diagnosis and cyber--physical monitoring further motivate a precise benign-fidelity definition \cite{ma2024,alcaraz2024,wu2023}. Turning, slip, stale state, or model mismatch can all enlarge physical--virtual divergence without an attack. This paper therefore establishes benign operational fidelity first; it does not present an attack detector.

\section{Service-Relative Operational Fidelity}
\subsection{Physical--Virtual Discrepancy}
Let a physical asset expose application-relevant quantities
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_p(t)=[z_{p,1}(t),\ldots,z_{p,M}(t)]^{\top},
\end{equation}
with corresponding twin quantities $\mathbf{z}_T(t)$. For component $q$, an application-appropriate discrepancy operator defines
\begin{equation}
D_q(t)=d_q\!\left(z_{p,q}(t),z_{T,q}(t)\right).
\label{eq:discrepancy}
\end{equation}
The discrepancy can be pose error, heading disagreement, temperature error, battery-temperature mismatch, or process-state mismatch. The framework does not require all domains to share one physical unit or one error operator.

\subsection{Operational Service Contract}
For service $s$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_s=(Q_s,H_s,\boldsymbol{\tau}_s,C_s,A_s),
\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
where $Q_s$ is the set of required quantities, $H_s$ is the service horizon, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_s$ contains tolerated discrepancies, $C_s$ is the context over which the claim applies, and $A_s$ is the maximum allowable age of the represented physical information.

Let $g_{q,H_s}(W_k)$ summarize discrepancy for quantity $q$ over service window $W_k$. A window satisfies the contract when
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_{s,k}=\mathbb{I}\!\big[&g_{q,H_s}(W_k)\le\tau_{s,q}\;\forall q\in Q_s,\\
&c_k\in C_s,\;\mathrm{AoI}_k\le A_s\big].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:windowvalidity}
\end{equation}
where the context term is omitted when no context restriction is imposed and the age term is omitted in experiments that do not manipulate or observe source age. When source timestamps are available,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AoI}(t)=t-t_{\mathrm{src}}^{\mathrm{newest\ represented}}.
\label{eq:aoi}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:windowvalidity} is a local decision. To connect it to the run-level quantities reported later, define service satisfaction over $N_s$ eligible windows as
\begin{equation}
S_s=
\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{k=1}^{N_s}V_{s,k}.
\label{eq:runsatisfaction}
\end{equation}
Thus $S_s=1$ means that every eligible service window in that run satisfies the specified contract, while intermediate values represent the fraction of eligible windows satisfying it.

Several experiments intentionally evaluate a prespecified tolerance grid $\mathcal{G}_s$ rather than one deployment threshold. For those sensitivity studies we report
\begin{equation}
\bar S_s=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_s|}
\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_s} S_s(g),
\label{eq:gridaverage}
\end{equation}
which we call \emph{grid-average contract satisfaction}. Equation~\eqref{eq:gridaverage} measures robustness over a sensitivity surface; it is \emph{not} itself a deployment qualification, a safety probability, or a universal fidelity score. A real deployment evaluates its actual contract $g^\star$ and may additionally require $S_s(g^\star)\ge\rho_s$ for an application-selected reliability target $\rho_s$. We do not prescribe a universal $\rho_s$.

The formulation intentionally permits
\begin{equation}
S_{s_1}\neq S_{s_2}
\end{equation}
for the same physical--virtual pair. This is the central distinction from an undifferentiated fidelity label.

\subsection{Contract Lifecycle and Violation Semantics}
Here ``contract'' denotes an engineering service specification and acceptance rule, not a legal service-level agreement. Its lifecycle is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Specify:} the consuming application chooses $Q_s$, $H_s$, the required tolerances, applicable context, and freshness requirement.
    \item \textbf{Qualify:} a frozen twin/configuration is evaluated on held-out or prospective physical data under that specification.
    \item \textbf{Monitor:} synchronized evidence is converted to $V_{s,k}$ and $S_s$ as service windows complete.
    \item \textbf{Withdraw or adapt:} a violation withdraws the fidelity claim for service $s$; the system may then degrade/disable that service, request fresher sensing, increase update resources, switch configurations, or fall back to a safer mode.
\end{enumerate}
For example, a stale global pose may invalidate a geofence service while leaving a short-horizon relative-motion service usable. The present paper evaluates specification, qualification, replay monitoring, and live requested-rate selection. It does \emph{not} show that requested rates caused distinct delivered sensing rates or improved downstream task performance.

\subsection{Tolerance Provenance and Qualification Boundary}
Three tolerance sources must be distinguished:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Application-defined limits}, derived from task, performance, or safety requirements;
    \item \textbf{Qualification-derived limits}, estimated only from calibration/training/benign reference data and then frozen;
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity-analysis grids}, used to test whether a conclusion depends on one arbitrary threshold choice.
\end{enumerate}
In this paper, the E1 i2Nav tolerance surfaces are prespecified sensitivity-analysis grids; E2 reuses the same robot grids unchanged on TerraSentia; and E3 uses one frozen normalized sensitivity grid for all three non-robot domains. No held-out result is used to choose a threshold that favors a twin. Accordingly, grid-average results quantify ranking/representation sensitivity and are not presented as certified deployment requirements.

\subsection{Mobile-Robot Instantiation}
For planar motion,
\begin{align}
\mathbf{x}_p(t)&=[x_p(t),y_p(t),\theta_p(t)]^{\top},\\
\mathbf{x}_T(t)&=[x_T(t),y_T(t),\theta_T(t)]^{\top}.
\end{align}
Synchronized global disagreement includes
\begin{align}
D_p(t)&=\sqrt{(x_p-x_T)^2+(y_p-y_T)^2},\\
D_\theta(t)&=|\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_p-\theta_T)|.
\end{align}
Finite-horizon relative motion is formed in $SE(2)$:
\begin{align}
\Delta T_p(t,h)&=T_p(t)^{-1}T_p(t+h),\\
\Delta T_T(t,h)&=T_T(t)^{-1}T_T(t+h),\\
E_R(t,h)&=\Delta T_p(t,h)^{-1}\Delta T_T(t,h).
\end{align}
Translational RPE is obtained from $\operatorname{trans}(E_R)$. We evaluate local services at $h\in\{1,5,10\}$~s and a synchronized-global service. The local services ask whether relative motion remains faithful over a bounded horizon; the global service asks whether the twin remains physically co-located with the asset.

Signed velocity/yaw-rate residuals, accumulated yaw mismatch, p95/max tails, and condition-dependent summaries are retained as diagnostics. They explain \emph{why} a contract fails without being collapsed into one weighted score.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Normalized Specialization}
Native physical tolerances cannot be transferred directly between temperature, battery, process-composition, and robot-pose variables. E3 therefore normalizes each paired quantity by a robust scale computed from the \emph{physical reference quantity}:
\begin{align}
s_q &= \max\!\left(P_{95}(z_{p,q})-P_{05}(z_{p,q}),\epsilon_q\right),\\
e_q(t)&=\frac{|z_{T,q}(t)-z_{p,q}(t)|}{s_q},
\label{eq:normerr}
\end{align}
where $\epsilon_q$ is a small magnitude-relative safeguard for near-constant channels. The scale never uses the virtual-model residual itself and therefore cannot be tuned to reduce a model's error. Because E3 is a retrospective structural-portability study, the physical reference record is used to define this descriptive scale; it should not be interpreted as a prospective certification threshold.

In each non-overlapping service window, $g_{q,H}$ is the p95 of $e_q$. The primary frozen protocol uses
\begin{align}
H&\in\{60,300,600\}\ \mathrm{s},\\
\tau&\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10,0.20,0.50\}.
\label{eq:e3grid}
\end{align}
The reported quantity is therefore the grid-average contract satisfaction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gridaverage} over a prespecified normalized tolerance surface. It compares service difficulty under a common representation; it is not raw physical accuracy, a safety probability, or a universal cross-domain score.

After freezing the primary $P_{95}$--$P_{05}$ protocol, Stage-1 publication hardening repeated E3 with $P_{90}$--$P_{10}$ and $1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}$ scaling without changing the horizon set or tolerance grid. All three datasets remained structurally non-degenerate under the alternative normalizations. These reruns are robustness checks only; the primary E3 table remains tied to Eq.~\eqref{eq:normerr}.

\subsection{Statistical Hierarchy}
Robot timestamps and windows within a run are temporally dependent. The hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
\text{timestamp}\subset\text{seed/run}\subset\text{physical sequence}\subset\text{dataset}.
\end{equation}
Dataset-level robot inference therefore uses the physical sequence as the primary unit. E1 uncertainty uses 10,000 sequence-level bootstrap resamples and deterministic leave-one-sequence-out analysis. The reported bootstrap intervals are percentile intervals. We do not bootstrap overlapping trajectory windows as if they were independent experiments. Timing replay uses a separate paired sequence-level bootstrap described in Section~\ref{sec:timing}.


\section{Twin Instantiation and Frozen Evaluation Layers}
\subsection{Sensor-Lightweight Twin Maintenance}
The mobile-robot twin is maintained from wheel/odometry and IMU streams. Its virtual state propagates corrected forward speed and yaw rate:
\begin{align}
\theta_{T,k+1}&=\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_{T,k}+\omega_{T,k}\Delta t),\\
x_{T,k+1}&=x_{T,k}+v_{T,k}\Delta t\cos(\theta_{T,k}+\omega_{T,k}\Delta t/2),\\
y_{T,k+1}&=y_{T,k}+v_{T,k}\Delta t\sin(\theta_{T,k}+\omega_{T,k}\Delta t/2).
\end{align}
The frozen V2 maintenance model separates transient correction from a bounded slow rotational correction. Ground truth is used for training supervision and held-out evaluation only. The architecture is not presented as localization state of the art; it provides a reproducible sensor-lightweight twin on which operational fidelity can be studied.

\subsection{Condition and Mechanism Diagnostics}
Different operating conditions stress different service dimensions. High wheel--IMU disagreement worsens RPE1 in 10/10 sequences; high turning worsens RPE5/RPE10 in 9/10; high acceleration worsens position-tail error in 9/10; and high curvature worsens heading-tail error in 10/10. Persistent signed yaw mismatch is strongly associated with accumulated yaw residual, while accumulated mismatch propagates to global position/heading in a trajectory-dependent way.

Conditioned q95 benign envelopes were also evaluated leave-one-physical-sequence-out. Conditioning changes contextual sharpness but does not uniformly improve coverage. We therefore retain conditioned envelopes as descriptive context rather than claiming a superior online service-risk predictor.

\section{E2: Cross-Platform Robot Contract Portability}
TerraSentia changes robot morphology, drivetrain, environment, and sensing distribution. E2 does \emph{not} ask whether frozen i2Nav V2 becomes a high-fidelity TerraSentia maintenance model. It asks whether the same service-contract construction remains computable and informative without target-domain threshold tuning.

All five accepted physical sequences satisfy the frozen data-quality gates. The unchanged 1/5/10-s and synchronized-global position contract yields 20/20 non-degenerate sequence/service surfaces, satisfying the structural-transfer criterion. Macro grid-average contract satisfaction for frozen V2 is 0.778, 0.418, 0.223, and 0.083 for local1, local5, local10, and global services, respectively.

\section{E3: Cross-Domain Contract Portability}
\subsection{Frozen Transfer Protocol}
E3 removes robot pose, ATE, RPE, wheel odometry, and $SE(2)$ geometry entirely. MAGNET pairs ten thermowell measurements with released DT forecasts \cite{wilsdon2023magnet,inlmagnetdata}. FreeTwinEV pairs experimental 1S4P electro-thermal measurements with released identification/validation multiphysics simulation outputs \cite{freetwinevmeas2026,freetwinevsim2026}. Setpoints and references are excluded as physical truth.

The same horizon set and six-value normalized tolerance grid are used in all three domains. A structural PASS means that the unchanged representation produces complete, non-degenerate contract surfaces at all required horizons with sufficient contract units. \section{Synchronization Sensitivity for Edge/IoT Twins}
\label{sec:timing}
A DT deployed across an embedded/edge boundary depends on the temporal consistency of physical and virtual streams. We perturb the timing relationship between the saved physical trajectory and the already-generated frozen V2 virtual-state stream. This is a controlled \emph{virtual-stream synchronization replay}: it is not a packet-level wireless-network simulation and does not delay raw IMU/odometry inputs into the learned correction model.

Fixed update delay is evaluated at 0, 25, 50, 100, and 200~ms using causal zero-order hold of the delivered virtual-state stream. Timestamp jitter is zero-mean Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
\delta t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),
\qquad
\sigma\in\{0,10,25,50\}\ \mathrm{ms},
\end{equation}
with nonzero-jitter user seeds 0--4 and a deterministic per-file seed term so different trajectories do not share identical random jitter patterns. Jitter replicates are first averaged within each original run/checkpoint, run/checkpoint results are then averaged within each physical sequence, and the physical sequence is the paired inference unit. Confidence intervals use 20,000 paired sequence bootstrap resamples with seed 42 and the percentile interval (2.5th, 97.5th percentiles).

At 50-ms jitter, the mean absolute RPE1 increase is 0.0405~m with 95\% percentile CI [0.0308, 0.0498]~m, while the ATE increase is only 0.00203~m [0.00098, 0.00322]~m. The service implication is explicit: under the same timing degradation, a twin may remain acceptable for a global tracking contract yet violate a short-horizon monitoring/control-support contract, or vice versa. A single global trajectory score therefore cannot determine timing adequacy for every consuming service.

The frozen V2 checkpoint is small (approximately 0.16~MiB serialized), and its six-feature 10-Hz fast branch corresponds to roughly 240~B/s of model-side feature values before protocol overhead. These values establish interface feasibility only. They are not substitutes for measured network bandwidth, energy, end-to-end latency, or AoI.


\section{UGV01 Asset-Specific Physical Validation}
\label{sec:ugv01-physical-validation}

To complement the broad cross-sequence fidelity analysis on i2Nav, we evaluated
the instantiated twin on a real UGV01 tracked rover using overhead
AprilTag-referenced video and matched onboard telemetry collected on August 29,
2026. The physical reference used four fixed world tags and a rover-mounted tag
in a corrected $2.0\,\mathrm{m}\times1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ layout with tag ordering
$1=\mathrm{top\text{-}left}$, $2=\mathrm{top\text{-}right}$,
$3=\mathrm{bottom\text{-}right}$, and $4=\mathrm{bottom\text{-}left}$. A
matched $1280\times720$ ChArUco calibration was used to support
elevation-corrected reference recovery, so the resulting analysis measures
planar physical--virtual fidelity in a common local frame rather than
post-aligned trajectory agreement.

We first evaluated a baseline deterministic UGV01 twin and then fit an
asset-specific parameterization on the run
\texttt{WIN\_20260829\_15\_35\_55\_Pro}. The fitted configuration used an
effective track width of $0.192\,\mathrm{m}$ together with the current
low-speed gyro-fusion setting of $0.20$. This fitted instantiation was then
held fixed and assessed against two additional runs from the same day to test
cross-run transfer without retuning.

On the calibration/reference run, asset-specific fitting materially improved
physical--virtual fidelity over the full $164.7\,\mathrm{s}$ evaluated
trajectory. The baseline configuration achieved $0.297\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE,
$0.044\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and $35.6^\circ$ heading MAE,
whereas the fitted twin achieved $0.149\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE,
$0.024\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and $21.2^\circ$ heading MAE. The
fitted run also achieved a median position error of $0.099\,\mathrm{m}$, a
position-error p95 of $0.295\,\mathrm{m}$, and remained within
$0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ for $87.3\%$ of evaluated samples. This confirms that
binding the generic template to the specific UGV01 geometry and turning
behavior can substantially improve local physical--virtual agreement on the
target asset.

We then tested whether that fitted instantiation transferred to two additional
August 29 runs. On \texttt{WIN\_20260829\_16\_01\_14\_Pro}, the corrected
baseline twin achieved $0.284\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE,
$0.034\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and $28.8^\circ$ heading MAE,
while the frozen fitted twin degraded to $0.505\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE,
$0.062\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and $76.3^\circ$ heading MAE. On
\texttt{WIN\_20260829\_16\_15\_16\_Pro}, the corrected baseline twin achieved
$0.685\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE, $0.091\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and
$35.3^\circ$ heading MAE, whereas the frozen fitted twin degraded further to
$1.136\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE, $0.166\,\mathrm{m}$ RPE$_{1\mathrm{s}}$ RMSE, and
$108.7^\circ$ heading MAE. Thus, the fitted instantiation improves the
calibration run strongly but does not yet generalize robustly across repeated
holdout trials, particularly for heading-sensitive behavior.

This outcome is scientifically important rather than merely negative. It shows
that asset-specific instantiation is real and beneficial, but also that twin
fidelity remains condition- and run-sensitive even on the same physical robot.
In the best case, the UGV01 twin attains strong positional and short-horizon
agreement; however, heading fidelity remains weaker and transfers less
reliably. This directly supports the paper's central argument that digital-twin
fidelity is multidimensional and service-relative rather than a single scalar
notion of accuracy.

\section{Live UGV01 Contract and Resource-Policy Study}
\label{sec:live-ugv01}
\subsection{Protocol and Independent-Reference Boundary}
We executed 20 indoor smooth-floor runs using four frozen resource policies---static-low, static-high, AoI-only, and contract-aware---with five repetitions per policy. Each run has a telemetry CSV, a phone video, and a JSONL trace of online sensor age, virtual state, contract states, requested policy rate, response latency, and payload size. The audited package contains 2702 valid JSONL records over 29.9~min of live traces and 34.6~min of paired video. All 20 triplets are present; no JSON record is malformed; and seven unexplained sequence gaps represent 0.258\% of expected sequence steps. The runs test one physical condition and baseline Wi-Fi, not a factorial surface or wireless-stress campaign.

GPS was the \emph{operational online reference} for the live service decisions, not independent ground truth. The separate AprilTag experiments in Sec.~\ref{sec:ugv01-physical-validation} provide the physical reference for fidelity. Valid GPS position packets were present in the telemetry exports, but low-speed GPS course often failed the heading-observability gate. Consequently, the 1-, 5-, and 10-s position-plus-heading services were mostly unobservable. Such windows are neither contract passes nor failures. The global position service was more often observable, but its GPS--twin disagreement commonly exceeded the frozen tolerance. No claim that a policy maintained qualified finite-horizon service follows from these data.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{The Contract Makes Metrics Actionable}
The central result is not that ATE or RPE are inferior metrics. ATE is well aligned with synchronized-global fidelity; RPE is well aligned with finite-horizon relative-motion fidelity. The ambiguity appears when a DT is assigned one undifferentiated fidelity label without stating the service. The contract supplies the missing semantics:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{application requirement}
&\rightarrow \text{measurement + horizon}\\
&\rightarrow \text{tolerance/context/freshness rule}\\
&\rightarrow \text{qualification / violation}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A weighted scalar can encode one fixed tradeoff, but it cannot preserve every service-specific decision simultaneously when local and global rankings reverse. Service-relative evaluation therefore organizes existing measurements rather than replacing them.

\subsection{What a Contract Violation Means}
A violation does not imply that the entire DT is ``bad.'' It means that the evidence no longer supports the specified service under its current contract. A global-tracking violation may coexist with acceptable short-horizon motion fidelity, and vice versa. Operationally, a consuming system should withdraw or degrade only the affected service claim and then apply an application-specific response: request fresher sensing, increase update resources, switch configurations/models, or fall back to a safer mode. The live policy implements the requested-rate response, but the measured transport prevents a claim that this response changed fresh delivered sensing or downstream task quality.

\subsection{Framework Portability Is Not Model Portability}
TerraSentia is important precisely because it prevents an overclaim. The contract transfers unchanged, but frozen i2Nav V2 does not become a high-fidelity TerraSentia twin merely because it executes there. A portable computational representation becomes an asset-specific twin only after it is bound to a physical asset and qualified for the services being claimed.

E3 pushes this boundary further by removing ATE, RPE, robot pose, wheel odometry, and $SE(2)$ geometry. The same quantity--horizon--tolerance logic remains computable on thermal forecasts, a battery electro-thermal measurement/simulation pair, and an industrial process twin. This supports \emph{cross-domain structural contract transfer}; it does not establish universal threshold transfer, universal operational adequacy, or cross-domain model superiority.

\subsection{Online Cost and Edge-System Implications}
Once the discrepancy summaries $g_{q,H}$ are available, evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:windowvalidity} requires one bounded comparison per required quantity plus the context/freshness checks, i.e., $O(|Q_s|)$ decision work per completed service window. The contract layer therefore does not add another learned inference model. Maintaining exact rolling maxima/quantiles has implementation-dependent memory and update cost, so we do not substitute asymptotic complexity for measured embedded runtime.

The timing experiment nevertheless links operational fidelity directly to IoT resource design. Different services tolerate stale state differently, motivating the policy objective
\begin{equation}
\pi(\mathcal{C}_s)
=
\arg\min_r \mathrm{Cost}(r)
\quad
\text{s.t.}\quad
P(V_s=1\mid r)\ge \rho_s,
\end{equation}
where $r$ represents sensing rate, communication conditions, and compute budget. The live UGV01 study implements the requested-rate decision but shows that transport capacity can prevent that decision from changing delivered updates. Therefore, the optimization remains a design objective rather than a demonstrated optimum: communication or energy savings require a transport path that realizes distinct rates and a reference that keeps the relevant service observable.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Claim Freezing}
The primary i2Nav study uses the frozen full-LOSO V2 source state associated with commit \texttt{6540c01f90f3c1074de0d8dae9964a5276fbbc91}. Stage-1 hardening does not retrain the twin or change E1/E2/E3 primary thresholds. E1 uncertainty resamples physical sequences; E3 normalization variants are post-freeze sensitivity checks; and the timing analysis perturbs only the saved virtual-stream timing relationship. The UGV01 release contains 20 policy/trial directories with paired video, telemetry, and JSONL traces, plus machine-readable audit and replay summaries. These layers are kept distinct so measured online behavior is not conflated with counterfactual transport replay or hypothetical capacity bounds.

\subsection{Implications for Security Monitoring}
A divergence-based security detector that treats large physical--virtual disagreement as inherently malicious is unsafe when benign turning, slip, model mismatch, or stale state can create the same symptom. Service-relative fidelity provides the benign operational baseline required by a later security study, but no attack-detection performance claim is made here.

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations remain. First, E1 has only ten independent physical sequences, so ranking uncertainty remains substantial despite threshold-surface and leave-one-sequence-out robustness. Second, TerraSentia fused EKF heading is a secondary reference rather than independent ground truth, and its motor/IMU semantics limit causal attribution of large global drift. Third, E3 uses dimensionless normalization for structural comparison; its contract-satisfaction values are not probabilities of correctness or physical-unit safety margins. Contract-unit counts and temporal dependence also differ substantially across the three non-robot domains, so E3 does not support pooled cross-domain performance inference. Fourth, conditioned benign envelopes are descriptive and were not shown to be a superior online risk predictor. Fifth, the timing study perturbs frozen virtual-state timing rather than raw network packets or sensor inputs. Sixth, UGV01 asset-specific fitting improves the calibration run but degrades on two frozen holdouts, particularly in heading. Finally, the live campaign covers one indoor physical condition and baseline Wi-Fi; low-speed GPS course limits finite-horizon observability, the serial HTTP path suppresses delivered-rate separation, no energy measurement is available, and the replay cannot establish counterfactual service satisfaction.


\section{Conclusion}
This paper formulates digital-twin fidelity as a service-relative operational contract rather than an undifferentiated scalar property. The framework connects required physical--virtual quantities, service horizon, tolerance, context, and information age to window validity, run-level satisfaction, and an explicit qualification/violation decision. Frozen i2Nav experiments show that local and synchronized-global service rankings can reverse across prespecified tolerance surfaces, while established ATE and RPE metrics align most strongly with the services matching their semantics. The same contract logic transfers structurally to TerraSentia and, through one frozen normalized specialization, to thermal, battery electro-thermal, and biomass-to-SNG DT data. Timing replay shows that service horizons respond differently to synchronization degradation. AprilTag-referenced UGV01 evidence demonstrates both asset-calibration gains and frozen cross-run limitations. Finally, 20 live UGV01 runs verify online contract and policy execution while revealing that a serial transport bottleneck collapses distinct requested rates into approximately 1.5 delivered updates/s. The contract therefore makes explicit \emph{which service is supported, by what evidence, under what operating and freshness requirements, and which physical, observational, or communication constraint invalidates the claim}.
