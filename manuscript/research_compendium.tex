\begin{abstract}
This source is an evidence compendium for drafting a new paper, not a submission-ready claim that every experiment belongs in one journal article. It consolidates the completed repository studies of a sensor-lightweight mobile-robot digital twin, a service-relative fidelity framework, and sampled-delivery qualification. Ten i2Nav physical sequences and three seeds per sequence support the primary frozen-twin analysis; each sequence's own V2 trajectory is generated by a checkpoint trained without that sequence, but the later qualification-label holdout is not end-to-end sequence exclusion across every upstream checkpoint. AprilTag-referenced UGV01 recordings, five TerraSentia sequences, a twenty-run live UGV01 dataset, and independent thermal/process datasets provide distinct supporting or boundary evidence. After correcting saved-clock arrival comparisons, the cross-sequence qualification surface falsely qualifies 5/235 accepted cells versus 75/200 for service identity at approximately matched pooled acceptance. Paired replay distinguishes 30 delivery-remediable cases, six persistent cases, and four already qualified cases across ten sequences and four services. The compendium preserves unfavorable and exploratory results and separates independent physical references from fused estimates, measured live throughput from simulated capacity, and thermal forecast-target agreement from causal delivery semantics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A digital twin is useful only to the extent that its virtual state supports a particular decision at a particular time. A robot can have plausible one-second relative motion while its global pose has drifted far from the physical asset. Conversely, a current state may be physically accurate at its source time but too old for a fast control or monitoring service. This project began with UGV01 telemetry, sensor calibration, GPS/IMU/encoder fusion, and security replay, then developed a broader question: how should physical--virtual fidelity be measured, characterized under changing conditions, and qualified for different services?

The strongest paper direction is not a new odometry leaderboard, a new GPS-spoofing detector, or a universal update scheduler. It is a qualification-label-held-out and remediability protocol for frozen twins. The protocol asks whether a specified service is supported under a sampled delivery configuration on a physical sequence whose qualification labels were withheld from surface fitting, and whether a failed service could be restored by delivering newer \emph{already available} virtual states. The distinction matters because network delivery cannot repair drift in the underlying model.

The central independent units are physical sequences, not samples, replay cells, random seeds, services, or source phases. This compendium uses those units to organize both positive and negative results. It is intentionally broader than the concise IoTJ manuscript in \texttt{main.tex}; a future author should select rather than paste every appendix result into a submission.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
Digital-twin credibility and fidelity depend on intended use, validation context, and uncertainty \cite{jones2020,shao2023}. Trace-alignment fidelity and mobile-robot path-comparison methods provide complementary behavioral and geometric views \cite{munoz2024,bergs2025}. Neither a scalar trajectory RMSE nor a trace match alone establishes that all downstream services are valid. The project retains conventional ATE, RPE, and heading metrics and asks how they map to explicit service requirements.

Freshness work distinguishes the age of information from the age of incorrect information and from query-time freshness \cite{maatouk2023,chiariotti2022}. Digital-twin synchronization has also been formulated as a control or constrained allocation problem \cite{tanmatta2024,li2025sync}. The present protocol does not solve that optimization. It evaluates measured physical--virtual discrepancy, observability, and represented-state age for a fixed source trajectory under causally replayed delivery. Its kinematic-staleness comparator is literature-informed, not a reproduction of Li \emph{et al.}'s multi-object method.

Wheel--inertial odometry and learned correction methods motivate the lightweight twin, but the project does not claim to outperform camera/LiDAR systems \cite{brossard2019,jiang2025,zhou2025ynet}. i2Nav provides ten physical sequences for trajectory-level LOSO and qualification-label holdout \cite{tang2025}; TerraSentia provides a deliberately difficult external platform \cite{cuaran2024}. AprilTag UGV01 footage supplies an independent physical reference where available. MAGNET thermal data test whether fidelity decompositions can be meaningful beyond robot pose, with a crucial restriction: forecast target time is not necessarily forecast issue or arrival time \cite{wilsdon2023magnet}.

\section{Unified Methodology}
\subsection{Twin and reference}
The frozen sensor-lightweight twin propagates a planar pose from wheel/odometry and inertial motion, with V2 corrections learned on i2Nav and frozen before the final ten-sequence leave-one-sequence-out (LOSO) analysis. Runtime inference uses wheel/odometry and IMU, not GNSS, camera, LiDAR, radar, or ground truth. Reference pose is used for training supervision or held-out evaluation, not as a runtime V2 feature. Alternative fixed-physics trajectories are retained as paired configuration sensitivity, not additional independent robots.

\subsection{Componentwise fidelity}
For physical pose $p=(x_p,y_p,\theta_p)$ and virtual pose $T=(x_T,y_T,\theta_T)$ on a common time/frame, define
\begin{align}
D_p(t)&=\sqrt{(x_p-x_T)^2+(y_p-y_T)^2},\\
D_\theta(t)&=|\operatorname{wrap}(\theta_p-\theta_T)|.
\end{align}
ATE summarizes global position discrepancy. RPE$_H$ compares relative motion over horizon $H\in\{1,5,10\}$ s. Speed/yaw-rate disagreement, persistent signed yaw residual, and accumulated yaw residual provide mechanism diagnostics. These components stay in physical units; no arbitrary weighted meter-degree score is required. ATE after an official benchmark's SE(3) alignment is a separate metric from unaligned physical--virtual synchronization.

\subsection{Benign context and envelopes}
Frozen, outcome-independent condition bins cover speed, acceleration, turning, curvature, wheel--IMU disagreement, and elapsed-time regime. Within each seed run, componentwise empirical distributions are computed; seeds are aggregated inside physical sequence before cross-sequence inference. A descriptive 95th-percentile envelope $Q_{0.95}[D_j\mid c,H_0]$ is not an attack detector or a safety limit. Its usefulness must be assessed by held-out coverage, width/sharpness, and sensitivity to hard sequences.

\subsection{Service contract}
A service $s$ specifies required quantities $Q_s$, horizon $H_s$, componentwise tolerances $\boldsymbol\tau_s$, applicable context $C_s$, and maximum age $A_s$. The observable contract outcome is pass if all required limits hold, fail if an observable limit is violated, and unobservable if reference or history support is insufficient. An unobservable window is an abstention, not a pass. Run-level satisfaction divides passing windows by observable windows and is always reported with observability.

\subsection{Delivery replay and qualification-label transfer}
From a saved 10-Hz source stream, each specified rate samples source states. An added delay sets arrival time; at evaluation time the receiver represents the newest \emph{arrived} source state under zero-order hold. Source and arrival times are compared on an integer-nanosecond representation of the saved clock so floating-point rounding cannot add a whole 100-ms delivery tick. Age is measured from the original source time. The receiver never reads future physical truth or a future saved virtual state. However, V2's saved source trajectories include centered-gradient sensor features that can use the next sensor sample; the replay does not include that feature-availability latency. It is a delivery replay over precomputed states, not proof of fully online end-to-end causality. Four services (local 1/5/10 s and global) are crossed with four rates (10/5/2/1 Hz) and five nominal delays (0/25/50/100/200 ms). Physical, freshness, joint, and observability requirements remain separate. The 0.80 satisfaction and 0.95 coverage requirements are illustrative engineering settings, not certified safety standards.

For outer qualification-test sequence $u$, the cross-sequence surface is
\begin{align}
\widehat p_{-u}(s,r,\delta)
  &= \frac{1}{9}\sum_{v\ne u}q_v(s,r,\delta),\\
q_v(s,r,\delta)
  &= \mathbb I\{\text{service }s\text{ qualifies on }v
       \text{ at }(r,\delta)\}.
\end{align}
The identity-only baseline replaces this cell-specific score by the training-nine qualification frequency for service $s$, constant across its delivery cells. Nested LOSO on the nine qualification-training sequences selects each method's acceptance cutoff using acceptance only, then freezes it for $u$. Its qualification labels and outcomes are excluded from that fitting and cutoff selection. However, checkpoints that produced the other nine trajectories may themselves have used $u$ during V2 training, normalization, or checkpoint selection. Thus this is a qualification-label holdout, not an end-to-end unseen-sequence evaluation of the entire two-stage pipeline; the possible upstream dependence has not been repaired or quantified. False qualification is false accepted cells divided by all accepted cells. Ten physical sequences are resampled as clusters for intervals.

\subsection{Remediability and evidentiary hierarchy}
The paired degraded condition is 2 Hz/200 ms; ``ideal available delivery'' is only the saved 10-Hz stream at zero added delay; the practical contrast is 5 Hz/50 ms. A case is delivery-remediable when degraded delivery fails but ideal available delivery qualifies, persistent when ideal still fails, and already-qualified when degraded passes. Held-minus-ideal discrepancy is descriptive; it is not an exact additive decomposition into model and network causes. Evidence is ranked: independent physical reference $>$ fused-reference diagnostic $>$ offline replay $>$ simulation $>$ exploratory pilot. A result never changes category merely because its number looks favorable.

\section{Official i2Nav Benchmark and Sensing Burden}
The verified official protocol uses TUM trajectories in the local NED convention, 5-ms association tolerance, SE(3) alignment without scale correction, and translation/rotation APE plus all-pairs distance RPE at 50--300 m. Frozen V2's official translation APE macro mean is 1.635 m and rotation APE macro mean $3.011^\circ$; 50-m RPE translation macro mean is 1.310 m. Fixed-physics official translation APE macro mean is 3.299 m. The V2 macro-mean reduction is 50.4\%, whereas mean sequence-wise relative reduction is 34.9\%; these are distinct estimands. V2 improves official APE on 9/10 sequences. Official alignment compresses some long-horizon drift and must not replace the unaligned physical--virtual fidelity evaluation. V1 scalar internal results are available, but exact matching official-format V1 trajectories were not found, so no official V1 score is invented.

Runtime V2 uses wheel/odometry and IMU only, with 40,675 state elements, an approximately 0.162-MiB serialized state dictionary, and a 10-Hz update rate. No inference latency, power, or network bandwidth was measured by the model-resource audit. In the audited official comparison, stronger camera/LiDAR systems remain more accurate; a favorable sensing burden is contextual positioning, not Pareto optimality.

\section{Alternative Fidelity Evaluators and Model-Development Studies}
Conventional ATE/RPE, Bergs-style path geometry, Mu\~noz-style trace alignment, and componentwise Twin Fidelity Profiles answer overlapping but different questions. An aligned-behavior score may tolerate timing differences that a synchronized state-service cannot, while Hausdorff-type geometry can ignore when the rover occupied a point. The six-method comparison includes fixed physics, sparse-RBF adaptation, V1, V2, wheel--IMU EKF, and a reduced-input yaw+EKF adaptation; results should not be collapsed into a single ``best fidelity'' rank. Several alternatives have very low local RPE yet poor global ATE, reinforcing the local/global distinction.

Earlier i2Nav learned-$Q$ and model-bakeoff experiments compared MLP, boosted trees, random forests, and related uncertainty predictors using GPS-independent features. On parking00, quantile gradient boosting and extra trees have lower normalized covariance-target MAE than the MLP, but downstream EKF consistency remains a separate test. These are development/model-selection results, not the frozen V2 timing-paper outcome. The nested causal service-risk estimator was a predeclared \emph{no-go}: history features did not beat simpler risk baselines across the required services/sequences. Thus the manuscript should not imply a superior online risk predictor.

\section{UGV01 Live Prototype and Transport Limits}
The final live-contract dataset has four requested-rate policies and five trials each, with 20/20 video/telemetry/JSONL triplets, 2,702 JSONL records over 30.09 minutes on the rover source clock, and zero JSON parse failures. The condition is smooth-floor mixed motion with baseline Wi-Fi, not a factorial surface/wireless campaign. Requested rates differ (2 Hz static-low, 10 Hz static-high, mean 2.77 AoI-only and 8.61 contract-aware), but corrected delivered response rates cluster at 1.41--1.52 Hz; the serial HTTP path is the bottleneck. The old dashboard truncated elapsed time across long sample gaps, compared unrotated twin and GPS positions, and left qualification stale during outages. Code repairs now use true elapsed time, a common planar frame when heading can be anchored, and stale-state abstention; these repairs were unit-tested but were not deployed during the twenty recorded runs. Historical GPS-discrepancy and contract-state fields must therefore not be presented as corrected live validation. The logged AoI is relative to a minimum observed offset, not verified absolute transport age. Low-speed GPS course also leaves finite-horizon position-plus-heading services mostly unobservable.

Five measured contract-aware traces were reused for a trace-driven serial-HTTP replay under four policy rules. Under recorded service time, every policy yields about 1.536 requests/s, 951.8 response bytes/s, and 1.304-s p95 delivery AoI. A separate fluid capacity sensitivity shows potential offered-demand separation at provisioned 10 updates/s, but that is simulated demand, not observed savings. Repeated source samples do not become fresh observations. Position-only GPS replay is an observability sensitivity, not independent physical AprilTag validation.

\section{Cross-Domain Fidelity, MAGNET, and Timing Stop Gate}
An unchanged normalized quantity--horizon--tolerance contract is computable and nondegenerate on MAGNET thermal forecasts, FreeTwinEV electro-thermal data, and TU Wien SNG process data for frozen 60/300/600-s target windows. This is structural portability, not common physical-unit safety thresholds or model superiority. The MAGNET hardened forecast-fidelity analysis has 23 nonoverlapping paired windows; median RMSE at within-window target-time offsets 0--60 s is 0.0876~$^\circ$C and at offsets 301--599 s is 6.6176~$^\circ$C, with a median paired increase 6.0425~$^\circ$C (95\% interval 2.2828--6.6952). Similar aggregate RMSE can hide tail/persistence/target-offset differences. Overlapping windows are not treated as independent replicates.

Crucially, the MAGNET virtual file records forecast \emph{target} time, not verified forecast issue/availability time. The provenance audit found no defensible mapping to an arrived forecast at a live evaluation instant; therefore no causal MAGNET delivery, AoI, or remediability analysis was run. \section{Task Decision and Other Null Results}
A causal position-only geofence replay used a simulated 1-Hz independent physical reference delayed 0.2 s, while full truth remained evaluator-only. At the illustrative 1-m/1-s point, always-use released nearly all answers with roughly 0.3\% error; the full contract released about 46\% with roughly 1.1\% error. Sparse occupancy and few boundary crossings undermine a prospective task-protection claim. The full contract is exactly equivalent to error-plus-freshness under the available gates. This null result should prevent using the paper to imply task-level safety benefit.

Timing delay and timestamp-jitter diagnostics are distinct: delay affects availability of a saved virtual state, whereas jitter perturbs reported timestamps. Any plot using these interventions must identify whether it is causal online replay, offline interpolation sensitivity, or fluid capacity simulation. Neither a plotted trend nor a lower requested rate constitutes measured communication or energy savings.

\section{Historical Security Replay: Separate Research Track}
The original project investigated GPS manipulation and residual-coupled covariance adaptation. In archived offline replay, 20 physical runs, three start times, and attack profiles yielded 1,440 unique attack--run--start combinations; a later 14-detector table has 20,160 detector-run evaluations. Those detector rows are not independent physical attacks. A targeted paired covariance analysis finds naive residual-coupled adaptation can increase process uncertainty, suppress normalized innovation scores, and increase undetected paired divergence relative to controls. For standard drift against fixed covariance, the pooled NIS ratio difference is $-0.251$, and maximum undetected paired error is 0.668 m larger. These are counterfactual offline replay effects measured against a clean paired twin, not overhead physical ground truth, live compromise, or part of the present timing-paper thesis. The evidence gate is often inactive in these rows; the archived results do not prove a universally superior composite detector.

\section*{Extended Conclusion and Writing Guidance}
The most defensible new-paper thesis is a narrow but substantive one: service qualification for a frozen IoT digital twin should be judged against physical--virtual evidence at the service's quantity, horizon, and freshness requirements, and the transfer of each sampled delivery cell should be tested with that physical sequence's qualification labels withheld. This does not imply exclusion of the sequence from every upstream checkpoint used to construct the other training trajectories. The cross-sequence qualification surface adds pooled information beyond service identity at approximately matched acceptance. The remediability ledger then prevents a common interpretive error: better communication can expose newer already available virtual states, but it cannot fix persistent model divergence. That distinction survives a second legitimate frozen twin configuration, while its numerical counts are sensitive to the service requirement, source clock, phase, and receiver reconstruction.

The deepest empirical insight predating the timing paper is the local/global separation. parking02 and the UGV01 square show that low finite-horizon RPE and large global drift can coexist. Operating conditions alter the distribution of discrepancy, but conditioned envelopes have mainly descriptive/sharpness value rather than a uniform coverage advantage. The official i2Nav benchmark provides protocol-compatible positioning, not a substitute for unaligned fidelity. TerraSentia demonstrates that the evaluator and service contract can transfer structurally even when the frozen maintenance model does not. AprilTag ground truth supports direct physical comparison, while the UGV01 holdouts show that a pleasing calibration run is not sufficient for a general asset claim.

The project also has meaningful negative evidence. The live UGV01 transport collapsed requested policies to nearly the same delivered rate, so resource saving is unproven. Low-speed GPS course often made live heading-dependent contracts unobservable. The geofence replay did not demonstrate task protection. MAGNET's target-aligned thermal forecast errors are interpretable, but missing forecast issuance and availability times prohibit a causal timing replay. The service-risk predictor and condition-aware support envelope did not broadly beat simpler alternatives. Preserving these limits makes the positive timing result more credible.

Several methodological principles emerge. First, the reference must be independent enough for the claim: AprilTag pose can assess UGV01 physical trajectory fidelity, while a fused EKF heading in TerraSentia is only a secondary diagnostic. Second, temporal provenance matters as much as spatial accuracy. A target timestamp on a forecast does not tell a live receiver when that forecast was issued or delivered. Third, comparisons must use the same evaluand. Official i2Nav SE(3)-aligned APE is a benchmark result; unaligned $D_p(t)$ is a synchronization result. Treating one as the other would erase precisely the accumulated divergence under study.

The statistical design is also part of the contribution. Three V2 seeds quantify algorithmic variability for a held-out physical sequence; they do not triple the number of independent robot trajectories. Twenty replay cells per service explore protocol sensitivity but do not create 20 new physical experiments. The bootstrap therefore resamples ten sequences. When a score is discrete, a target acceptance fraction can be unattainable; the correct response is to disclose achieved acceptance and withhold a matched per-service claim. The same discipline applies to repeated source phases, multiple tolerance settings, and paired twin configurations.

The case accounting is the cleanest practical explanation of the method. A service that fails at 2 Hz/200 ms but passes when every available 10-Hz source state is delivered has a delivery-remediable failure under this protocol. A service that still fails at 10 Hz/0 ms has persistent discrepancy that transport improvement alone cannot resolve. A service already qualified under degraded delivery needs no proposed delivery change for that case. The corrected 30/6/4 partition is exhaustive, and the 27 practical recoveries are a subset of the 30, not a separate sample. At this operating point, the remediable cases fail discrepancy even while passing the evaluated freshness rule. Changing delivery cannot fix the underlying physical model.

For an eventual systems paper, the live implementation gap is concrete. The recorded serial HTTP path cannot deliver the policy's requested 2--10-Hz rates, and GPS course is frequently not observable at slow indoor speed. A stronger downstream systems claim would require a transport path that realizes distinct rates, matched trials on the same motion, an appropriate live physical reference, and a measured benefit in bytes, latency, power, or task decisions. These are not hidden in the existing 20 runs. The present data instead show why a qualification protocol should abstain or withdraw unsupported claims.

For an eventual fidelity-method paper, the most valuable additions would be more independent physical conditions under a frozen model and a measured reference error budget. The carpet calibration and smooth-floor examples already demonstrate useful boundaries, but the August 29 holdouts show why same-run fitting is not external validation. A rigorous prospective UGV01 experiment would freeze geometry, sensor conventions, synchronization, and service thresholds before collection; then evaluate position, heading, rate, and local/global services on unseen runs. The compendium should help design that experiment, not imply it has already happened.

For a concise IoTJ article, lead with the causal protocol, cross-sequence surface versus service identity, complete remediability accounting, and frozen-configuration sensitivity. Use the official benchmark, UGV01, TerraSentia, MAGNET, and historical security replay only to establish scope and boundaries. This compendium is intentionally maximal; it is not evidence that every archived pilot or figure should be promoted into the final paper. Future claims of online policy savings, safety, or general cross-platform model portability require new evidence rather than rewording existing traces.
