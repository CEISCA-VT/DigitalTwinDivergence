\subsection{UGV01 Asset-Specific Physical Validation}
\label{subsec:ugv01-physical-validation}

To complement the broad cross-sequence analysis on i2Nav, we evaluated the
instantiated twin on a real UGV01 tracked rover using overhead AprilTag video
and matched onboard telemetry collected on August 29, 2026. After re-auditing
the reference setup, we corrected the world-tag ordering to match the physical
layout used during collection: tag $1$ at the top left, tag $2$ at the top
right, tag $3$ at the bottom right, and tag $4$ at the bottom left of the
$2.0\,\mathrm{m}\times1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ rectangle. We then recalibrated the
camera with a matching $1280\times720$ ChArUco clip and re-ran elevation
correction for the $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ AprilTags with a rover-tag center height
of $0.09112\,\mathrm{m}$. The corrected solve yielded a physically plausible
camera height of approximately $1.48\,\mathrm{m}$ and mean fixed-tag
reprojection errors below $5$ pixels on all three August 29 runs.

We first evaluated a baseline UGV01 configuration and then calibrated a
deterministic asset-specific parameter set on the run
\texttt{WIN\_20260829\_15\_35\_55\_Pro}. The fitted parameterization used a
distance scale of $0.95$, clockwise effective track width of $0.17\,\mathrm{m}$,
counterclockwise effective track width of $0.28\,\mathrm{m}$, and gyro-fusion
weight of $0.20$. These parameters were then frozen and applied without
retuning to two additional August 29 runs to test whether the same
instantiation generalized across repeated physical trials.

On the calibration/reference run, the matched-calibration/elevation-corrected
fitted twin materially improved fidelity relative to the baseline over the full
$164.7\,\mathrm{s}$ trajectory. The baseline achieved $0.297\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE
RMSE, $0.044\,\mathrm{m}$ 1\,s RPE RMSE, and $35.6^\circ$ heading MAE, whereas
the fitted twin achieved $0.149\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE, $0.024\,\mathrm{m}$ 1\,s
RPE RMSE, and $21.2^\circ$ heading MAE. The fitted run also achieved a
position-error p95 of $0.295\,\mathrm{m}$, remained within $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$
for $87.3\%$ of evaluated samples, and reduced encoder-only ATE from
$0.234\,\mathrm{m}$ to the stated corrected-twin result.

We then tested transfer on two additional runs from the same day using the
frozen fitted parameters. On \texttt{WIN\_20260829\_16\_01\_14\_Pro}, the
baseline outperformed the frozen fitted variant: baseline ATE RMSE was
$0.284\,\mathrm{m}$ versus $0.505\,\mathrm{m}$ fitted, 1\,s RPE RMSE was
$0.034\,\mathrm{m}$ versus $0.062\,\mathrm{m}$, and heading MAE was
$28.8^\circ$ versus $76.3^\circ$. On \texttt{WIN\_20260829\_16\_15\_16\_Pro},
baseline again remained stronger: $0.685\,\mathrm{m}$ ATE RMSE versus
$1.136\,\mathrm{m}$ fitted, $0.091\,\mathrm{m}$ 1\,s RPE RMSE versus
$0.166\,\mathrm{m}$, and $35.3^\circ$ heading MAE versus $108.7^\circ$. The
updated UGV01 evidence therefore shows two things at once: asset-specific
calibration can materially improve fidelity in the calibration regime, but
that improvement does not guarantee frozen cross-run validity.

These UGV01 results reinforce the central claim of the paper that fidelity is
multidimensional and cannot be stamped onto the twin once and for all. In the
best case, the instantiated twin achieved strong position and short-horizon
motion agreement under a corrected physical reference. However, repeated runs of
the same rover still exhibited materially different frozen-transfer outcomes.
This means that service qualification must remain tied to the measured evidence
for the current operating run, rather than to a permanent assertion that the
twin is simply ``accurate.''

\paragraph{Reference and synchronization limitations.}
The present UGV01 validation is stronger than the earlier provisional version
because it now uses a matched-resolution ChArUco calibration and a physically
consistent elevation correction. However, the analysis still uses motion-based
correlation to estimate the video/telemetry time offset rather than a dedicated
hardware-visible synchronization pulse, and GPS is absent from these three
physical-validation runs. Accordingly, the current UGV01 section should be
interpreted as credible AprilTag-referenced physical-validation evidence for the
fidelity paper, while a future live end-to-end contract study is still needed
for measured resource and AoI claims.
